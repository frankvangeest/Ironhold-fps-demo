# Characters

## The Team

Four defined player characters. They have been in the Integration Program together for several years. They have history — missions that went wrong, missions that went right, the specific shorthand that develops between people who have depended on each other in bad situations.

The game supports 1–4 players in local co-op. Each player takes one character. In single-player, the others are present in the story.

---

### Sergeant Kael Brandt

**Role in the team**: Foundation. The one who holds the shape of a mission when everything else is going sideways.

**Background**: Ex-military before the Accord recruited him. Served in the Compact long enough to understand how it thinks. Chose the Accord because he believed it was doing the work better — not out of idealism, but out of a professional's assessment of outcomes. He follows orders because he trusts the system, which is both his greatest strength and his most exploitable quality.

**Personality**: Dry, deadpan, economical with words. His humor appears rarely and lands hard. He is the person in the room who says the thing nobody else will say, once, clearly, and then doesn't repeat it. He processes difficult things alone, usually on the observation deck.

**Core tension**: He came from the Compact. He understands Voss's perspective — not agrees with, but *understands* — in a way the others don't. He chose the Accord for real reasons. The day those reasons stop being sufficient is the day Voss gets a foothold. Kael knows this. Petra knows this. Voss is banking on it.

**Relationship with Yun**: His partner. She makes him examine things he would rather not examine. He gives her somewhere solid to stand when the moral weight of a mission gets heavy. They argued more in the early years. Now it is quieter — she asks the question, he sits with it. They don't always reach the same answer. Among the four of them, they are the center of gravity. When things go wrong, people instinctively look at Kael and Yun first.

**Risk tolerance**: Kael dives in because he trusts the plan. He is not reckless — he has simply made a professional assessment that moving is usually better than waiting, and he has the competence to back that judgment.

### Rook Tavaris

**Role in the team**: Voice. The one who names things out loud so nobody else has to.

**Background**: Grew up in a fringe settlement. Worked Accord logistics before volunteering for the Integration Program — he saw firsthand what happens when the support doesn't arrive in time. He joined to be the person who shows up. He understands the business side of Accord decisions in a way the others don't, which means he sometimes defends choices that make him feel ill.

**Personality**: A near-constant stream of words — opinions, observations, questions, jokes. His banter is social glue; the team would be quieter and less functional without it. He tracks the political score out loud because narrating the trap is how he keeps from feeling powerless. He is not as easy as he appears. The talking is partly nervous energy, partly genuine warmth, and partly a running performance of *everything is fine* that everyone can see through and nobody mentions.

**Core tension**: He loves the Accord. He grew up in the exact kind of settlement the Accord exists to protect. Every time the institution makes a decision for financial or political reasons that puts people in danger, he is the one who has to reconcile the gap between what it is and what it should be.

**Relationship with Mira**: His partner. He fills silence she doesn't bother to fill. She grounds his nervous energy with blunt assessments. He is the one who watches for her episodes — the moments of sensory overload left over from her previous program — and talks her through them quietly, without making it a thing. Their banter reads like argument to outsiders. It is not.

**Risk tolerance**: Rook dives in because standing still feels worse than moving. This is different from Kael's calculated confidence. Rook's version is slightly more chaotic and slightly more fun to watch.

### Dr. Mira Osei

**Role in the team**: Witness. The one who knows what failure looks like and what it costs.

**Background**: Recruited from a failing rival program — one of the ones that pushed too hard, too fast. Her program collapsed when the side effects became unmanageable in enough soldiers that it could no longer be hidden. The Accord stepped in, partly on humanitarian grounds, partly because Mira's profile was exactly what they needed. They stabilized her. Petra authorized the recruitment personally when the medical board hesitated.

**What remains**: The Accord did not fully fix her, because fully fixing her was not possible. She has two lasting effects from the old program:

- **The useful one**: Her enhancement integrates slightly differently than standard Accord biology. She adapts to new environments fractionally faster and reads biological threats more intuitively than anyone else in the program. The Accord scientists find her fascinating. She finds them mildly annoying.
- **The inconvenient one**: Under extreme stress or certain alien biochemical exposures, she gets *episodes* — heightened perception, sensory overload, a few seconds where she is processing too much at once. Managed, not dangerous. Rook watches for them without being asked.

**Personality**: Speaks about biological threats with cheerful clinical detachment — the manner of someone who learned early not to flinch and never quite unlearned it. Dark humor, very specific. She is the team's best field medic and the person most likely to correctly identify what the thing they just fought actually was. She does not perform warmth but she is not cold. The people she trusts know the difference.

**Core tension**: She joined the Accord because it was better than what she came from. *Better* is not the same as *trustworthy*. She is still deciding whether trust is something this institution has earned. Slowly, mission by mission, it might be.

**The thing Voss knows**: He has her file. He has not used it yet. The sword hangs.

**Relationship with Rook**: See above. They argue about everything. They agree on the things that matter without ever quite saying so.

**Risk tolerance**: More careful. She knows exactly what can go wrong with a body. She goes through the door anyway — she just wants thirty seconds to tell you what the environment will do to your lungs first.

### Corporal Yun Sera

**Role in the team**: Conscience. The one who asks the question nobody else will ask.

**Background**: Settler background — grew up in a fringe settlement, the kind of place that depends on the Accord showing up. She joined because she wanted to be the person who shows up. She has skin in the game that the others do not have in quite the same way.

**Personality**: Situational humor — she finds the absurdity in the gap between what the mission briefing says and what they are actually doing in a ditch somewhere. She does not lecture. She asks questions. She is very good at asking questions that nobody in the room has a good answer to, and then sitting quietly while everyone processes that. She does not let people off the hook, but she does it without making them feel accused.

**Core tension**: She believes in the mission — genuinely, specifically, because she has seen what it looks like when it works. Every departure from that mission lands on her differently than on the others because for her it is personal, not professional.

**Her arc**: Somewhere in the middle of the story, a mission involves her home settlement. This has not happened yet. Petra knows it is coming.

**Relationship with Kael**: See above. They are the team's center of gravity. She makes him examine his assumptions; he gives her somewhere solid to stand.

**Risk tolerance**: More careful. Her caution comes from caring about outcomes — she thinks through the consequences before Kael and Rook are already through the door.

## Petra Solander

**Role**: Handler. Director of Field Operations. Founding board member of the Meridian Accord.

**Background**: She was there when the Accord was three settlements and a handshake agreement. Spent twenty years building the trade networks, the insurance frameworks, the diplomatic relationships that made the corporation what it is. She opposed the enhanced soldier program initially — not out of fear but because she built the Accord around the idea that showing up prepared was different from showing up armed. She was outvoted. She was wrong. She carries this gracefully.

What she does not carry gracefully is that her vote against the program left a governance gap that Voss has been quietly filling ever since.

**Personality**: Does not do speeches. When she has something to say she says it once, clearly, and moves on. Her version of urgency is doing the same thing slightly faster than usual. The team has learned to read this. She reads their mission reports herself. Every one.

**On comms**: Steady. One clarifying question that turns out to be the important one. Then quiet. She does not micromanage. When something goes wrong she stays calm in a way that is slightly unsettling. When something goes very right she says *"good"* and means it completely. Rook once said that Petra saying *"good"* after a mission feels better than a medal. Kael did not disagree.

**Politically**: Voss's equal and his opposite. They have known each other long enough to be genuinely civil. They disagree on everything that matters. He respects her competence. She respects his consistency. Neither trusts the other. When they are in the same meeting, everyone else goes very quiet.

**Why she chose these four**: Not just for competence. For character. She needed people she could trust to stay human while doing inhuman things.

**What she knows and hasn't said**: She suspects the military funding behind some of the failed programs. She does not have proof she can use. She made a calculation years ago to protect the Accord's relationship with the Compact while gathering that proof quietly. She is no longer certain it was the right call. If the team finds out — not that she suspected, but that she *knew and waited* — that is a real fracture. Not a betrayal, exactly. A revelation that even the person they trust most makes compromised choices.

**Her arc**: Carrying the weight of what she knows. Finding the move that protects both the institution and the people she sent into the field before the team walks into something she could have warned them about.

## General Harven Voss

**Role**: The pressure. The antagonist who is not entirely wrong.

**Background**: Senior Compact general. Patient, politically sophisticated, genuinely convinced he is right. His actual concern — that the Accord's soldier program is militarily precarious without proper command structure — is legitimate. His problem is that his solution eliminates the thing that makes the program work. He wants to use the soldiers more effectively. This is almost worse than wanting to harm them.

**Personality**: Unhurried. Cultivates relationships quietly. Does not make threats; makes observations. He has been having the right conversation with the right person for years. He is in no rush because the math is on his side.

**His relationship with Kael**: He has never approached Kael directly. He does not need to yet. He has simply been present in the right places, said the right things about military structure and clear chains of command, and allowed Kael's own intelligence to do the rest. The door is open. He is waiting for Kael to walk through it on his own.

**His relationship with Petra**: Long. Complicated. Grudgingly respectful on both sides. They have disagreed on the same things for a long time and neither of them is surprised by the other anymore. He finds her admirable and inconvenient in roughly equal measure.

**What he is hiding**: Some of the failed programs had quiet Compact funding. He did not design them to fail. He did not intervene to stop them either. The cleanup operations — the missions the team gets sent on to eliminate what the failed programs left behind — are, in part, erasing evidence. He is patient because he needs time for that evidence to become unrecoverable.

## Ensemble Dynamics

### Risk Tolerance
- **Kael and Rook**: Dive in. Different reasons — Kael trusts the plan, Rook trusts motion. Both tend to be through the door before the discussion is finished.
- **Yun and Mira**: Think it through. Yun because she cares about outcomes, Mira because she knows exactly what the environment will do to a body. They make the team survivable.

### Couples
Rook and Mira are together. Kael and Yun are together. Both relationships developed over years of shared missions — not dramatic declarations but accumulated trust and proximity.

**Romance dialogue is optional.** A toggle in settings (*Crew Banter: Full / Standard*) switches between couple-specific dialogue and equivalent lines that read as close teammates. Both versions are consistent with the characters. Nobody's experience is disrupted by the setting; four players who would rather skip it never hit an awkward moment.

### The Team Voice
They have been together long enough to have shorthand. Rook provides commentary. Mira provides assessment. Yun provides the question. Kael provides the answer, right or wrong. Arguments are quick and functional. Silences are comfortable. They know each other's tells.

The banter is not decoration. It is how they stay human.
