# Act 1: The Good Work

## Theme

*What we are vs. what we're becoming.*

Five missions. The first two show the team at their best, doing what the Accord is supposed to do. The third introduces the discomfort. The fourth plants the seed. The fifth changes what the story is.

---

## Mission 1 — *Survey*

**Location**: Maren's Moon
**Type**: Escort / Environmental
**Suit**: Core

The team escorts an Accord survey crew assessing the moon for resource extraction viability. Routine. The briefing does not mention that the territorial herd predators have migrated to the survey zone since the last flyover, or that the quarterly swarm event is running three days early this season, or that the survey team lead is the nephew of a Compact general and has the situational awareness of a filing cabinet.

Nothing in the mission goes as planned. Everything in the mission goes fine.

**Beats**:
- Rook flags the predator migration signs within five minutes of landing. Nobody in the survey crew listens.
- Mira identifies the early swarm indicators before the survey team's equipment does. She tells the survey lead. He thanks her and continues setting up in the wrong location.
- Yun's instinct that the briefing was optimistic is confirmed approximately every twenty minutes.
- Kael holds the shape of the mission through a territorial predator encounter, an early swarm, a survey equipment failure, and the survey lead making three decisions in a row that a more experienced person would not have made.
- Petra's voice on comms: one clarifying question at the start, silence through the chaos, *"good"* at the end.

**Tone**: Competent chaos. Funny in retrospect. The team is clearly very good at this.

**What it establishes**: Individual competence and team dynamic. The gap between mission briefings and field reality. Petra as a presence. The survey lead as an early minor antagonist who represents the gap between the Accord's ideals and the people those ideals sometimes have to protect. The Compact general's nephew is not a spy. He is simply oblivious, which is its own kind of problem.

## Mission 2 — *Home Fires*

**Location**: Drevet (fringe settlement)
**Type**: Rescue / Support
**Suit**: Core

A Drevet farming collective has a missing survey team — three people, overdue, last signal from the northern ridge. The team deploys. They find the civilians: one injured from a structure collapse, two managing, all three alive. The danger is real — local predators, unstable terrain, one person who needs medical stabilization before transport — but the stakes are clear and the outcome is good.

When the team returns the civilians, the settlement turns out food and a table and they eat with strangers who are genuinely grateful. Yun is quiet in a good way. Rook talks too much. Mira accepts a cup of something unidentifiable and drinks it without comment. Kael thanks the settlement coordinator with exactly the right number of words.

**Beats**:
- The settlement coordinator (recurring minor character) meets the team for the first time. She is competent, direct, and quietly grateful in a way that doesn't perform itself.
- Rook asks Mira what she's drinking. She says she doesn't know. She finishes it anyway.
- Yun and Kael have a quiet moment on the transport back. She says something like: *"This is the job."* He agrees. They both know it won't always look like this.

**Tone**: Warm, with real danger. The Accord at its best. The mission that reminds everyone why they signed up.

**What it establishes**: Emotional stakes — these are the people they exist to protect. Drevet as a recurring location. The settlement coordinator as a recurring face. Yun's connection to places like this, which will matter later.

---

## Mission 3 — *Political Weather*

**Location**: Unnamed industrial platform, outer Reach
**Type**: Faction elimination
**Suit**: Core

Petra's debrief before this mission is clipped. A Compact liaison *recommended* — that word, precisely — that the Accord operatives handle a situation. A rogue faction has seized an Accord-adjacent resource platform and is holding the crew. The Compact's assessment is that lethal resolution is required. The Accord's preference would be negotiation. The platform crew doesn't have time for the Accord's preference.

The team goes. The faction is not entirely what the briefing said. Some of them are settlers who were promised ownership stake in the platform and had their contract voided by a legal technicality. Some of them are genuinely dangerous. The team handles it — minimum casualties, crew extracted, platform secured — and the outcome by the numbers is good.

In debrief, Yun asks the question: *"When does this stop being ours and start being theirs?"* Kael says the mission was clean. Rook stares at the ceiling. Mira files her after-action report in silence.

Rook, later, mentions that the Compact liaison who made the recommendation had General Voss's office signature on the referral.

**Beats**:
- The faction leader is not a monster. This is the problem. He is a person who made bad decisions after being wronged, and now the team is the mechanism of consequence for both the wrong and the decisions.
- Kael handles this professionally. Yun handles it by not sleeping well.
- Mira's after-action report is precise and thorough and contains nothing about how she feels, which is its own kind of statement.
- Rook's observation about Voss's signature is delivered as an aside, half-joke. Nobody laughs.

**Tone**: Competent, uncomfortable. Not a disaster. That's almost worse. The first mission where the team does something they are not entirely sure they should have done.

**What it establishes**: Voss as an active presence, not just a political abstraction. The mechanism of the trap in practice. The team's differing responses to moral discomfort. Petra's position — she negotiated the mission down from what the Compact originally wanted, and the team doesn't know that.

## Mission 4 — *New Ground*

**Location**: Site Ocher
**Type**: Resource extraction setup
**Suit**: Core

The Accord's response to Mission 3 is to accelerate independence. The team secures and establishes Site Ocher as a new extraction staging point — the kind of mission that funds the program's autonomy and builds the wall between the Accord and Voss's jurisdiction. Practical, unglamorous, strategically important.

The installation is clean. The equipment is in order. The local fauna is manageable. Everything is fine.

Except there is a section of the installation that doesn't match the blueprints. Sealed. Recent power draw on an isolated circuit. Mira finds biological trace residue near the sealed access point — not local fauna. She does not recognize it. She does not say anything on comms. She takes a sample.

The mission completes. The extraction point is established. On the transport home, Mira tells Rook, quietly. He stops talking for almost four minutes. The others notice. He does not explain.

**Beats**:
- The sealed section is real and wrong but not immediately dangerous. The team doesn't push it — mission parameters don't cover unauthorized access to sealed infrastructure. Kael makes the call to complete the primary mission. It is the right call. It does not feel like it.
- Rook's four minutes of silence is the longest silence he has produced in years. Yun and Kael exchange a look.
- Mira's sample goes into her personal kit, not the mission manifest.

**Tone**: Routine with a wrong note underneath it. The calm before the thing you can't unsee.

**What it establishes**: The seed of the mystery. The sealed section, the unrecognized biological trace. The first time the team has information they haven't given to Petra — not from distrust but from not knowing what they have yet. The first hairline crack in the team's default transparency.

## Mission 5 — *Cleanup*

**Location**: A moon in the outer Reach, catalogued as uninhabited
**Type**: Alien infestation / Elimination
**Suit**: Core (Haz recommended on site; team adjusts)

Another Compact referral. A research outpost has gone dark — officially a seasonal swarm overrun. Standard enough to be routine. The team deploys to assess and clear.

It is not a seasonal swarm.

The creatures are wrong. Territorial, aggressive, adapted to the environment — but wrong. Mira identifies the enhancement markers in the biology before she says anything out loud. These are not animals. They were people, once. The modification is a generation old, rough, failed — consistent with early enhancement programs, the ones that pushed too hard and didn't ask enough questions. The ones that had funding that wasn't fully disclosed.

The outpost is not a research station. It is a decommissioned program facility. Compact insignia, covered badly. Decommissioned approximately three months ago — not long enough.

The team completes the mission because people are in danger and the work in front of them is real. On the way home the transport is quiet for a long time.

Kael says: *"Petra needs to know."*
Yun says: *"Does she already?"*
Nobody answers.

**Beats**:
- Mira's episode. The biochemical environment triggers it — a few seconds of overload, Rook talking her through it quietly, then back. The others see it and say nothing because nothing is the right thing to say.
- The Compact insignia under the paint. Rook finds it. He photographs it without comment and keeps moving.
- Kael is very controlled on the way home. The kind of controlled that means he is working something through.
- Yun's question lands differently on each of them. Kael: possible. Rook: probable. Mira: she doesn't answer because she is thinking about what she found at Site Ocher.

**Tone**: The mission that changes what the story is. Competent execution of something that should not have been necessary. The moment when the cleanup operations stop being about alien infestation and start being about something the Compact wants not to exist anymore.

**What it establishes**: The mystery is no longer abstract. The military funding of failed programs is real and recent and being actively covered. Mira's past is suddenly very present. The team's trust in the system — including Petra — is no longer passive. It is a question they are actively asking.

---

## End of Act 1 — *The Debrief*

Petra calls them in privately. Not a standard debrief room. She closes the recording.

She tells them she has suspected something like Site Ocher for a long time. She tells them she does not have proof she can use yet. She tells them she needs them to keep doing the work — the real work, the Accord's work — while she finds the move that matters.

Then she tells them something she hasn't told anyone. Not everything. Enough.

Yun asks: *"How long have you known?"*

Petra says: *"Long enough that I should have told you sooner."*

The act ends there. The team is still the Accord's best. The mission is still the same mission. The trap is visible now, and Petra is the one holding the door, and the question that follows them into Act 2 is whether that makes her trustworthy — or whether it means she always knew the room they were walking into, and let them walk anyway.
