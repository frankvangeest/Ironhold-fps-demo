# World: The Reach

## Setting

Humanity has spread across several star systems — not many, not comfortably. The settlements are too spread out to call it civilization, too established to call it exploration. **The Reach** is the collective term for everything humanity has touched.

The inner systems are stable, governed, mapped. The fringe is where things get complicated — where the support structures are thin, the distances are real, and the difference between a settlement surviving or failing often comes down to whether someone shows up in time.

This fragility is not incidental. It is the reason the Meridian Accord exists.

---

## The Meridian Accord

A cooperative of settlements and nations. Founded by seven founding settlements pooling resources for mutual insurance, support, and trade. Now spans roughly forty settlements across nine systems. Publicly traded across member settlements — every settlement that joins gets a stake. Governed by a cooperative board representing all member settlements.

**What it does**: Insurance, logistics, trade facilitation, medical support, and emergency response across the Reach. The corporation shows up when things go wrong and helps things go right before they go wrong.

**What it is not**: A military organization. This distinction is increasingly difficult to maintain.

**Internal shorthand**: *the Accord*. Soldiers use it affectionately. Critics use it pointedly.

**Founding principle** (written by Petra Solander): *Not soldiers, but people who can go where others cannot.*

### Why It Can't Be Easily Absorbed

The Accord is publicly owned across forty boards of directors and forty sets of voters. Any attempt to absorb or nationalize it requires navigating all of them simultaneously. This is by design. It makes the corporation slow to change and very hard to take. Voss is patient because he doesn't need to absorb it all at once — he only needs to control the part that matters.

## Vantis Station

Orbital above **Calder**, the Accord's founding planet. Calder is a mid-tier world, not glamorous. The station orbits it because the founding settlements agreed on it as neutral ground, and neutral ground is easier to agree on when nobody particularly wants it.

The station is large enough to feel like a small city: medical wings, trade floors, administrative levels, research labs, residential blocks for long-term staff and their families. The enhanced soldier division occupies one wing. They share a cafeteria with procurement officers and terraforming consultants.

Vantis has a reputation for good coffee and terrible bureaucracy. Both are accurate.

The station is home — not just a base. This matters. The soldiers share corridors with people who have families, complaints about the ventilation, opinions about the cafeteria menu. The stakes are real because the place is real.

## The Compact

The multi-system military organization that predates the Accord and has never entirely reconciled itself to being outpaced by it. Legitimately funded, legitimately necessary, legitimately less effective than the Accord at the kind of work the Reach actually requires.

The Compact operates on a command model: clear authority, clear jurisdiction, clear use of force. In stable systems this works well. In the fringe it produces results that are effective in a narrow sense and costly in every other sense.

**General Harven Voss** is the Compact's most relevant political actor in relation to the Accord. He is not its highest-ranking officer, but he is its most patient one. See *Characters*.

## The Political Trap

### The Competence Snare

The Accord is being undermined by its own excellence.

The pattern runs as follows: a situation in the Reach requires a response that is more forceful than standard support operations. The Compact's approach would work, after a fashion, with acceptable casualties. The Accord's team handles it with fewer casualties and better outcomes. The public notes this. Political parties that want fewer military incidents point to the Accord as the preferred option. The Compact notes that the Accord is now performing military functions and argues this falls under military jurisdiction.

The Accord cannot win by succeeding. It cannot win by refusing — people die, which is politically catastrophic and personally unacceptable to the people doing the work. The only exit is to stop being the best option, which these people will never do because they genuinely care.

Voss is patient because the math is on his side.

### The Counter-Move

The Accord's strategy for resisting this is to accelerate independence: new resource extraction operations, new trade routes, new settlements they have helped establish and therefore have relationships with. Every brick of institutional independence is a brick in a wall between the soldier program and Voss's jurisdiction.

This means the team's most unglamorous missions — escort a survey crew, secure an extraction site, establish a staging point — are also the most strategically important ones. They don't always know this. Sometimes it helps not to.

## The Alien Situation

Most alien life encountered in the Reach falls into one of two categories:

**Territorial fauna**: aggressive, often organized into herd or swarm behavior, highly adapted to their environments. Not intelligent in any communicable sense. Some species have seasonal swarm events that make certain regions temporarily impassable. These are well-documented where settlements have been long enough to observe them; less well-documented on newly charted worlds.

**Intelligent hostiles**: not alien. Human — hostile factions, separatist groups, corporate rivals, Compact-adjacent actors pursuing their own agendas. The most unsettling "alien" threats the team encounters turn out to have human origins and human decisions behind them.

The intersection of these two categories — what happens when human enhancement programs incorporate alien biology without adequate understanding — is where the story's real darkness lives.

## Key Locations

### Maren's Moon
A resource-flagged moon two systems out, semi-charted. Named by the surveyor who first mapped it; the name stuck because nobody filed the paperwork to change it. Seasonal predator migrations, early swarm events, no permanent settlement. Where the story begins.

### Drevet
A fringe settlement, three thousand people, agricultural, barely viable. Exactly the kind of place the Accord exists to protect. A settlement coordinator who will become a recurring minor character. Yun knows places like this the way you know a word in your first language — without having to think about it.

### Site Ocher
A decommissioned survey installation repurposed as an extraction staging point, two systems from Vantis. Something at Site Ocher does not match the blueprints. The sealed section has recent power draw. Mira found biological trace residue she does not recognize. She took a sample. She has not filed it yet.
