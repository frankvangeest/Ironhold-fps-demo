# Act 2: Old Ground

## Theme

*How deep does it go, and what will it cost to find out.*

Act 1 ended with proof that the pattern is real. Act 2 is about turning that into evidence that can survive contact with people who don't want it to exist — while General Voss applies pressure on three fronts at once: Kael, Mira, and Petra. None of it looks like an attack. All of it is.

---

## Opening — *The Briefing*

**Location**: Vantis Station, Petra's private office
**Type**: No suit, no combat

Petra doesn't call a standard debrief. She calls the four of them, alone, and closes the door herself.

She tells them what she told them at the end of Act 1, and then more. Fragmented funding records she's pulled quietly over several years. A pattern of program collapses that all trace back, eventually, to accounts that shouldn't be able to reach into military budgets but do. She doesn't have enough to take to the board. She doesn't have enough to take to anyone. What she has is time running out — Site Ocher and the moon from *Cleanup* are both being scheduled for "decommissioning," which in Accord paperwork means demolition.

She asks them to keep doing their real work, and to look harder at everything adjacent to it than their orders technically require.

**What it establishes**: The team is now investigators as well as operatives, on every mission, without it ever being the mission. Petra has made herself vulnerable to them on purpose — trusting them with something that could end her career if it reached the wrong person. The four of them are, for the first time, fully inside the thing she's been carrying alone.

---

## Mission 1 — *Home Ground*

**Location**: Corrin's Landing (Yun's home settlement)
**Type**: Escort / Security assessment
**Suit**: Core

Routine, on paper: a Compact "security assessment" team has requested Accord cooperation at Corrin's Landing, citing elevated fauna activity near the settlement's perimeter. The team escorts the assessment and keeps the peace.

It is Yun's home. She knows the perimeter better than the assessment team does. She knows within an hour that the fauna activity is real but minor — nowhere near what would justify a permanent security presence, which is what the assessment is actually recommending. The Compact wants a garrison at Corrin's Landing. The settlement council doesn't want one. Yun is standing in the middle, in an Accord uniform, watching people she grew up around get told what's good for them by people who've been there for six hours.

**Beats**:
- Yun's aunt still lives here. She feeds the whole team without asking if they're hungry, then asks Yun, quietly, whether the Accord is going to let this happen.
- The assessment lead is not hostile, just certain. He has numbers. The numbers are technically accurate and functionally misleading — a classic Compact move, and Yun recognizes the shape of it before Kael has to explain it to her.
- Rook, for once, doesn't fill the silence. He watches Yun work and says almost nothing the entire mission.
- Kael backs Yun's read over the assessment's recommendation, on record, to Petra. It's the first time in the story he overrules a Compact-adjacent judgment instead of accommodating it.
- The mission resolves with the fauna threat handled and no garrison recommended — a win, but a narrow one, and everyone knows it's temporary. Corrin's Landing is now a place the Compact has decided to keep looking at.

**Tone**: Personal, quiet, angry in a controlled way. The political trap made small and specific and impossible to ignore.

**What it establishes**: The Compact's territorial pressure isn't abstract anymore — it has Yun's aunt's face. Kael choosing Yun's judgment over Compact numbers, on record, is a small early crack in the trust Voss is counting on. Corrin's Landing becomes a place the story will return to.

## Mission 2 — *Old Ground*

**Location**: Thessaly Point, outer Reach
**Type**: Infiltration / Evidence recovery
**Suit**: Haz

Following the trail from Mira's Site Ocher sample, Petra's quiet research points to Thessaly Point — officially an inactive relay station, actually still drawing power on the same signature Mira found at Site Ocher. The team goes in low-profile, no Compact notified.

It is the real thing: an old program facility, not fully stripped, records intact enough to be dangerous to somebody. Mira recognizes fragments of the methodology. This is close to where her own program operated, or built on the same research. The team recovers partial data — not proof of funding, but proof of continuity between programs that were supposedly unrelated and independently failed.

On the way out, at the extraction point, General Voss is there. Alone, unarmed, entirely reasonable. He tells Kael — just Kael, a few paces apart from the others — that he's read Kael's service record twice and that men like them end up understanding each other whether they mean to or not. He doesn't ask for anything. He says he'll be around. Then he leaves.

**Beats**:
- Mira goes quiet in the archive room in a way Rook has only seen once before. She doesn't have an episode. She just recognizes too much.
- The data they recover is real but incomplete — enough to know they're right, not enough to make anyone else believe them yet.
- Voss's conversation with Kael lasts less than ninety seconds. Kael doesn't repeat it to the team. He tells Yun, later, alone, on the transport. She doesn't tell the others either — not yet.
- Rook photographs everything he can before they leave. Mira tells him to stop touching things. He doesn't stop.

**Tone**: Tense, procedural, then suddenly very personal in its last minute. The mission that makes Voss a person in the room instead of a name on a referral.

**What it establishes**: Concrete evidence of program continuity, still short of proof. Voss's first direct approach to Kael — quiet, patient, exactly as his profile promised. The first secret inside the team since Site Ocher: Kael and Yun now know something Rook and Mira don't.

## Mission 3 — *Old Debts*

**Location**: Vantis Station / off-station meeting, arranged quietly
**Type**: Investigation, low combat
**Suit**: Core

A message reaches Mira through a channel that shouldn't still work: someone from her old program, a mid-level administrator who survived the collapse by disappearing early, wants to meet. She goes, against Petra's standing advice about unverified contacts, with Rook insisting on coming and Mira insisting he stay out of sight.

The administrator isn't selling information. He's scared. He says people connected to the old funding have been asking after former program staff — not to help them, to account for them. He says Mira's name has come up. He says he doesn't know who's asking, only that whoever it is has Compact clearances that shouldn't exist for a civilian inquiry.

He also says, almost as an aside, that her file was never fully sealed the way the Accord told her it was. Someone has had read access for years.

**Beats**:
- The meeting goes sideways when the administrator is confirmed to have been followed. It's not an ambush — nobody comes for them — but it means somebody is now aware Mira took the meeting.
- Rook, who was supposed to stay hidden, does not stay hidden the moment it looks like Mira might be in danger. She is furious. She is also not surprised.
- Mira tells Petra everything, this time, immediately. It costs her something to do it. Petra doesn't ask why she went. She asks what he said about the file.
- Petra confirms, quietly and unhappily, that she has known for over a year that Mira's file was accessed outside authorized channels and did not tell her. Another small fracture, smaller than Site Ocher, in the same wall.

**Tone**: Paranoid, close-quarters, personal in a way that has nothing to do with combat. The mission where the threat is entirely about who's watching whom.

**What it establishes**: Voss (or someone working for him) has had access to Mira's file for years — the sword Act 1 said was hanging is confirmed still there, and now the team knows it. A second, smaller instance of Petra knowing and not saying, which she owns immediately rather than being caught in.

## Mission 4 — *Quorum*

**Location**: Vantis Station (board level) / field mission running in parallel
**Type**: Political maneuver + escort, split narrative
**Suit**: Core

The Accord board convenes an emergency session to review "mission scope creep" in the Integration Program — officially about budget, actually a Compact-backed push to place a joint oversight liaison inside all field command decisions. It is the Competence Snare, formalized as a governance proposal.

The team is deployed on an unrelated, urgent escort mission during the vote — real, necessary work, not a manufactured distraction, but the timing is not a coincidence and everyone knows it. Petra fights the proposal alone in the room while the people whose competence is the entire argument for the proposal are unavailable to speak for themselves.

She wins — barely. The joint liaison proposal is not adopted. But it costs her a concession: an agreement that Compact liaisons may observe designated high-risk field operations "for coordination purposes," starting immediately.

**Beats**:
- The field mission itself is tense but clean — the team is good at this, which is, again, the whole problem.
- Rook narrates the political vote's likely outcome out loud during a lull in the mission, and gets the shape of it almost exactly right, which nobody finds funny.
- Petra's concession is presented to the team afterward as a win. It does not feel like one. Yun asks directly what "designated high-risk field operations" means in practice. Petra says she'll make sure it means as little as possible. Everyone hears the word *try* she didn't say.
- Kael, privately, understands the concession's real cost better than the others — he knows exactly what a liaison embedded in field command can become, because he's watched it happen from inside the Compact before.

**Tone**: Institutional, dry, quietly ominous. The mission with no monsters in it that is more frightening than the ones with monsters in it.

**What it establishes**: Voss's political front lands — not a defeat for Petra, but a wedge driven in under the guise of process. The mechanism (Compact liaison access to a field operation) that will make Mission 5 possible is now in place, and the team knows it's coming for "a" mission without knowing which one yet.

## Mission 5 — *Salvage*

**Location**: Thessaly Point, deep archive level
**Type**: Extraction under hostile conditions
**Suit**: Haz

The team returns to Thessaly Point to recover the archive core — the one piece of hardware that could turn "partial data" into proof. Because of the *Quorum* concession, a Compact liaison team is attached to the operation "for coordination." Petra fought to keep the attachment nominal. She did not entirely win that fight either.

The extraction goes wrong at the worst possible layer: the liaison team's presence means the operation's location and timing were known outside the Accord before the team ever launched. Someone was waiting. Not fauna. Not a rival faction. Professionals, minimal insignia, very clearly not there to negotiate.

Mira is the one who has to physically carry the archive core out of the vault — she's the only one who can read its interface fast enough to keep it from wiping under tamper response. In the chaos of the extraction, she gets cut off from the rest of the team by a collapsed access corridor. Kael, coordinating the retreat, hesitates for one critical stretch of seconds — some part of him running the math on whether the order forming in his head is his own or the shape of a conversation he had with Voss two missions ago. It's a small hesitation. It's enough.

By the time the team fights back to the corridor, Mira and the archive core are both gone.

**Beats**:
- The core itself is real, and its recovery — even incomplete, even lost — proves the site is worth killing to protect, which is its own kind of confirmation.
- Rook's reaction on realizing Mira is gone is the first time in the story he goes completely silent and stays that way.
- Yun holds the team together during the fighting retreat, and only afterward, standing in the wreckage, does she let herself react.
- Kael says nothing about his hesitation. Yun, who knows about Voss's approach, is the only one who understands exactly what just happened inside him, and she says nothing either — not yet.
- The Compact liaison team, notably, is nowhere to be found when the ambush hits, and reappears only for the extraction home, offering a version of events that doesn't quite match what the team experienced.

**Tone**: The mission the whole act has been building toward. Controlled chaos giving way to genuine loss. Not a cliffhanger fight — an ending.

**What it establishes**: All three of Voss's pressure fronts land in the same sequence: Petra's political concession created the opening, Kael's private hesitation cost the seconds that mattered, and Mira — the one whose file made her the most valuable and most exposed — is the one taken. Act 3 now has a concrete, personal, non-abstract objective.

---

## End of Act 2 — *The Empty Chair*

Back on Vantis, the debrief is short because nobody can make themselves extend it. Petra takes full responsibility for the concession in front of the team before anyone can raise it. It doesn't make the chair less empty.

Rook doesn't leave the debrief room after the others do. Petra stays with him, and for once says nothing at all, which is its own kind of answer.

Kael finds Yun afterward. He tells her, finally, out loud, exactly what Voss said to him at Thessaly Point the first time. She already knew. She listens anyway, because he needs to say it once, clearly, to someone — his own rule, turned on himself.

Nobody has to say what Act 3 is about. Get her back. Make the evidence survive this time. And find out, precisely, who among the people they answer to already knew this could happen and let the operation go forward anyway.
