# Act 3: Reckoning

## Theme

*What it actually costs to be the people who show up — and who has to pay it.*

Act 2 ended with Mira taken and the team's trust in the system fractured on three sides at once. Act 3 is the search for her, the confrontation with what Voss has actually been protecting, and the question the whole story has been building toward: when the door Voss left open for Kael finally opens all the way, does he walk through it.

---

## Opening — *No Contact*

**Location**: Vantis Station, mobile command
**Type**: No combat, planning

There is no clean search protocol for a captured Integrate. Petra pushes for one anyway, spending political capital she doesn't have much left of after *Quorum* and *Salvage*. She gets the team an off-books window before the board or the Compact liaison office can fold the search into "proper channels" — which everyone in the room understands to mean *slow it down until it doesn't matter.*

Rook is barely holding together and comes anyway. Yun flags that Corrin's Landing has gone quiet in a way she doesn't like — the security assessment team's tone has changed since *Home Ground*, and she thinks it's connected. Nobody has time to chase both threads properly. They're going to have to.

**What it establishes**: A ticking clock. Petra spending her remaining credibility on the team rather than the institution, for the first time without hesitation. Corrin's Landing seeded as a second front that will demand attention mid-act.

---

## Mission 1 — *Last Transmission*

**Location**: A Compact refueling depot on the transit route from Thessaly Point
**Type**: Investigation / infiltration
**Suit**: Void

Mira didn't go quietly, and she didn't go stupidly. Buried in her suit's last telemetry burst — sent automatically, not something anyone could have stopped without noticing — is a fragment she deliberately compressed into the data stream in the seconds before she was cut off: partial coordinates, and a status ping that reads, essentially, *I'm still thinking.*

The team traces the transmission to this depot, finds the transfer manifest for an "administrative transport" that doesn't match any Accord or public Compact registry, and gets a facility profile: hardened, remote, built for holding people who are not supposed to be found by anyone official.

**Beats**:
- Rook decodes the fragment personally, refusing to hand it to anyone else, and it's the first time since *Salvage* he looks like himself.
- The transfer manifest uses a requisition code the team has seen before — the same clearance signature Mira's old contact mentioned in *Old Debts*.
- Small, necessary hope: she was conscious, she was thinking clearly, and she left them a trail on purpose. Whatever else has happened to her, that part of her is intact.

**Tone**: Grim but propulsive. The first real ground gained since the loss.

**What it establishes**: A location fix on where Mira is likely held. Confirmation that the same clearance authority runs through her old program, her file access, and now her captivity — one thread, not three.

---

## Mission 2 — *The Door*

**Location**: A Compact courtesy vessel, neutral airspace
**Type**: No combat — confrontation

Voss requests Kael specifically, alone, "before this gets any worse for everyone." Kael goes, against Petra's advice and with Yun's blessing — she's the only one who knows this conversation was always coming.

Voss is calm, unhurried, and for the first time entirely direct. He confirms what the team has been assembling in fragments: he has known about the black-funded failed programs for years. He did not design them. He has, however, spent that time making sure their wreckage — the sites, the records, the things that used to be people — disappeared cleanly, routing the worst of the disposal work through operatives skilled enough to do it without a mess. Operatives like the Accord. Like Kael's own team, more than once, without their knowledge. He calls the total cost, without flinching, in the thousands. He calls it necessary. He has never reported any of it to the oversight bodies whose entire purpose is to know when the military does something like this, because — he says, and seems to genuinely believe it — some decisions are too important to survive being voted on by people who don't have to live with the consequences.

He offers Kael a version of the future where this all becomes simple: fold the Integration Program under real command structure, Kael in it, competence finally organized instead of politically hamstrung. Mira's situation, he implies without quite saying it, could resolve itself favorably as part of that conversation.

Kael tells him no. Once, clearly, the way he does everything that matters. He does not perform outrage — Voss would read outrage as weakness, and worse, as something to wait out. He simply says the arrangement isn't one he'll be part of, and that Mira comes back regardless of what Voss decides to do next.

Voss takes it well. That's the unsettling part. He says he expected as much, and that he's not in a hurry.

**Beats**:
- This is the scene the entire story has been walking toward since Act 1 — Kael understanding Voss's perspective without ever agreeing to share it.
- Voss's confession is delivered like a man explaining logistics, not confessing a crime. That's what makes it land.
- Kael's refusal costs him nothing on the surface and everything underneath — Yun, waiting for him afterward, is the only one who sees what it took.
- No threat is made. None is needed. Both men understand exactly where things stand when Kael leaves.

**Tone**: The quietest, heaviest scene in the story. No suits, no combat, no ambiguity left standing.

**What it establishes**: Full confirmation of the scale of the cover-up and Voss's deliberate concealment from political oversight — the concrete, prosecutable crime Petra has needed. Kael's answer to the question the story opened with. Voss's patience revealed as something closer to certainty that he's already won, which he has not.

## Mission 3 — *Undefended*

**Location**: Corrin's Landing
**Type**: Defense / escort under pressure
**Suit**: Core

The security assessment team at Corrin's Landing escalates to a formal garrison request, filed while the board's attention is entirely consumed by the search for Mira — the timing is either opportunism or retaliation for Kael's refusal, and it doesn't matter which. Yun won't let it happen undefended while she's able to stand in the way of it.

Petra backs her publicly, immediately, at real cost to what little political capital she has left. It is the clearest, least hedged thing Petra has done in the entire story.

**Beats**:
- Yun leads this one. Not by rank — by knowing every person and every reason this settlement matters in a way no one else in the room does.
- Rook, still raw from Mira, throws himself into this fight harder than the mission strictly requires. Kael lets him.
- The garrison request is withdrawn, publicly, after Petra makes it politically expensive to pursue. A real win, small and specific, exactly the kind the Accord has always been best at.
- Yun's aunt tells her, afterward, that she's proud of her. Yun, for once, doesn't deflect it.

**Tone**: A held breath released. The good outcome the story needs before it goes back down into the dark.

**What it establishes**: Petra rebuilding trust through action rather than explanation. Corrin's Landing's thread resolved — a home worth having, defended by the people who left it to make sure places like it stay standing.

---

## Mission 4 — *Extraction*

**Location**: Karst Hold, an unlisted Compact detention facility
**Type**: Infiltration / rescue
**Suit**: Haz

The team goes in on the fix from *Last Transmission*, no authorization, no backup beyond what Petra can quietly arrange. Karst Hold is exactly what its existence implies — built to hold people the system has decided don't need to be found.

Mira is alive. She has been questioned extensively and told them nothing usable, which is entirely in character and entirely the point — she has spent her whole life understanding institutions like this one, and she does not make their job easier. She is not unmarked, but she is not broken.

What the team also finds, in the facility's own intake records, is what Voss described in the abstract made concrete: names. Dozens of them at this site alone, spanning years, all cross-referenced to the same funding signature. Documentation, this time — not a fragment, not a partial trace. Proof that survives scrutiny.

**Beats**:
- The first thing Mira says when Rook reaches her is a joke, badly timed and completely deliberate, because she needs him to know she's still herself before either of them can afford to feel anything else.
- Kael tells her, on the way out, what Voss offered. He tells her before he tells anyone else, because she's the one it was actually about.
- The intake records are the single most important object the team has recovered in the entire story. Everyone treats them accordingly.
- The extraction is loud, contested, and successful. For the first time since *Salvage*, the team leaves a fight with everyone they came for.

**Tone**: Hard-fought relief. The rescue earns its catharsis, but the records they find on the way out make sure nobody mistakes this for the end of the story.

**What it establishes**: Mira recovered, changed but intact. Documentary proof of the cover-up's true scale — the evidence Petra has needed for years, finally real. Full team transparency restored: Kael tells Mira and, shortly after, the others.

---

## Mission 5 — *Reckoning*

**Location**: Vantis Station → the Council of Nine (the Reach's cross-settlement oversight body)
**Type**: Protect / escort, high stakes
**Suit**: Core

Petra bypasses the Accord board and the Compact chain of command entirely and requests emergency session with the Council of Nine — the body every member settlement answers to and the one Voss has spent years making sure never asked the right question. She and Mira carry the testimony. The team carries the records, and each other.

Voss's last move isn't an assault on the Council — he's too controlled for that, and he knows an attack would prove everything the records already show. Instead he moves to intercept the delivery quietly: a "routine" customs hold on the transport, a jurisdictional objection meant to delay the session past a procedural deadline, and — when that fails — a small, deniable team sent to make sure the physical records never arrive intact.

The team stops it. Not spectacularly. Precisely, the way they've always worked, because that's what the moment actually requires and they have never needed the situation to be dramatic in order to do the job.

The records reach the Council. Mira testifies. Petra testifies. The session runs long.

**Beats**:
- The interception attempt is a small, tense, close-quarters fight — the story's quietest action beat, and its most consequential.
- Kael faces, briefly, some of Voss's people in the field for the first time since *The Door*. He does his job. He does not enjoy it. He does not hesitate.
- Mira's testimony is delivered in her own voice, clinical and precise, and it is the most devastating thing in the story specifically because she refuses to perform grief for the Council's benefit. She just tells them what happened.
- Voss, informed the interception failed, does not run. He waits for the summons. That, too, is who he is.

**Tone**: Controlled to the edge and then, finally, resolution — earned rather than triumphant.

**What it establishes**: The evidence survives contact with the people who wanted it to disappear. Voss's mechanism — patience, competence, quiet control of the narrative — finally fails against a body he couldn't get to in time.

---

## Resolution — *What's Left*

Voss is not marched off in restraints. He's placed under formal Council inquiry and relieved of his command pending its outcome — the system working exactly as slowly and imperfectly as the story has always said it does. Some of his allies in the Compact will spend the coming months making sure the inquiry finds as little as it can. It won't find nothing. The joint-liaison proposal is withdrawn permanently; nobody has the appetite to revive it while his name is attached to it.

It is not the ending where the villain is destroyed. It is the ending where the door he was patient enough to leave open for years finally closes, because enough people decided, separately and at real cost, not to walk through it.

Back on Vantis:

- Mira returns to duty on her own timeline, not anyone else's. Rook doesn't leave much distance between them for a while. Nobody comments on it.
- Kael tells the whole team, fully, what Voss offered and what he said. It costs him to say it out loud a second time. It costs him less than the first.
- Petra sits with the four of them, off the record, and says the thing she's owed them since the end of Act 1: that she should have told them sooner, about all of it, every time. This time nobody has to ask.
- Yun, last, asks the question the story has been quietly asking since page one — whether the Accord can keep being the best option without it costing them more each time it's proven true. Nobody answers with certainty. They answer with a fifth mission requisition, already on Petra's desk, because the work doesn't wait for the question to resolve.

The founding principle holds, a little bruised, entirely intact: *not soldiers, but people who can go where others cannot.* They know now, precisely, what that has cost other people, and what it could cost them. They go anyway.

*(The other failed programs' darker experiments — the ones that incorporated alien biology, the ones with results even Voss couldn't control — remain unresolved. That thread was never Voss's to close.)*
